# Data Science Fundamentals write-up: Design

**Date:** 2026-08-11
**Deliverable:** `data/blog/data-science-fundamentals.mdx` (URL `/write-up/data-science-fundamentals`)

## Goal

A deep-guide write-up on data science fundamentals explained with Principal-level judgment, grounded in a study of what the 2025-2026 job market actually demands. Fills the site's gap in classical statistics, experimentation, causal inference, metric design, classical ML, evaluation, and Bayesian methods.

## Decisions (brainstormed with Ashim)

- **Angle:** fundamentals with a senior lens (judgment calls, failure modes), not advanced-only and not a pure market report.
- **Market study:** opening section with demand-tier tables and sourced numbers; it also determined which concepts got sections and their order.
- **Depth:** deep guide, Swiss Knife skeleton: hand-written TOC, numbered sections, no interview/Q&A framing.
- **No code (Ashim's revision):** the piece is conceptual; figures and tables carry the explanations, quantitative claims live in prose. Mermaid figures use classDef colors (stripped automatically in dark mode by `components/MermaidChart.tsx`), plus one `xychart-beta` bar chart for the peeking result.
- **Analogies:** minimal.

## Structure

Fundamentals-first (Ashim's second revision: the market data is confined to ONE section; the rest reads as "what a senior data scientist must know", plus interview prep):

1. Statistical Thinking Under Uncertainty (peeking bar chart, power arithmetic in prose, review-mistakes table)
2. Experiment Design (lifecycle figure, SRM, CUPED, pitfalls table)
3. Causal Inference Beyond the A/B Test (methods ladder, decision-tree figure, DiD and double ML in prose)
4. Metric Design and Decision Framing (metric-hierarchy figure, anti-patterns table)
5. Classical ML That Still Wins (defaults table, leakage taxonomy)
6. Evaluation and Calibration (thresholds and calibration in prose, evaluation-ladder figure)
7. Bayesian Decision-Making (frequentist-vs-Bayesian readout table, expected-loss stopping)
8. LLM Evaluation as a Measurement Problem (classical-to-LLM translation table)
9. LLM as a Judge (bias/mitigation table, position-swap protocol figure, four rules)
10. What Separates a Distinguished Data Scientist from an Average One (10-dimension comparison table, three deep dives)
11. What the Market Demands in 2026 (the one market section: ranked demand stack, methodology caveat, four facts)
12. Interview Prep for Senior Data Science Roles (loop table, question-archetype decoder table, metric-drop investigation tree figure, five senior signals)
13. Takeaways + Sources (market sources + methods references)

## Research basis

Web study run 2026-08-11 across ~19 sources (BLS, Indeed Hiring Lab, 365DataScience x2, Dawn Choo, Vourakis, Lightcast, Burtch Works, Stack Overflow 2025, Robert Half, LinkedIn, Interview Query, MIT NANDA, Netflix tech blog, TDS, Anaconda, O'Reilly, datanerd.tech). Key findings embedded in Section 1; every inline number carries its source, N, and year. Methodology variance (same publisher, Python 85% vs 57% depending on job board) is treated as a named finding: trust YoY deltas within one methodology, not absolute levels.
